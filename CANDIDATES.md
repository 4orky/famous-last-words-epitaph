# Candidate scoring

Fifteen original epitaphs, written for this bounty and scored 1–5 against
the judge's five criteria, most important first:

1. **Epitaph** — really a one-line tombstone epitaph of famous last words
2. **Laugh** — earns a real laugh on one read, a genuine joke
3. **Surprise** — turns on a surprise, a twist or a double meaning
4. **Stone** — reads like something carved on a stone: one short line, no
   stage directions, no explanation
5. **Memory** — stays in the head, quotable enough to repeat to a friend

## Winner

**#1 — "I can see the light. It's a train."** (24/25, 34 characters)

## Revision

The draft winner read "I can see the light. It's a train." Its one weakness:
"I can see the light" is the most worn dying cliché in the language, and
"can" softens the first beat. The revision tightens it to the canonical
phrase, dropping the filler word so both beats hit harder:

**"I see the light. It's a train."** (30 characters)

Shorter, punchier, more quotable — and the setup now sounds exactly like
the last words a person would actually say, which makes the twist land
harder. Nothing else changes: same joke, same two beats, four fewer
characters.
